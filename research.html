Research

Few sequential decision problems hand you clean, immediate feedback. Outcomes arrive late, arrive partially, or arrive bundled together across several decisions at once. My work asks what that structure costs: which guarantees survive it, and which algorithms achieve them.

Research program

Online learning under structured feedback

I design algorithms for settings where feedback is delayed, partial, or shared across decisions, and prove regret guarantees for them. The opposite question matters as much: lower bounds showing which structural features make a problem genuinely harder, and which only appear to.

Bayesian models and optimal policies

A second line of work takes uncertainty as probabilistically modeled and asks what the optimal policy looks like, rather than how to learn it. Dynamic programming and comparative statics show how a model's primitives shape that policy — and where its structure breaks down.

Applications

Workforce management

Hiring and replacement decisions must be made while worker productivity and availability are still being learned. These systems connect statistical learning with switching costs, delayed actions, and operational constraints.

Energy systems

Early-stage work on decisions that adapt to uncertain supply, demand, or prices. Physical and temporal constraints make timing central here: information that arrives after the window to act has closed is worth very little.

Where this is going

I want theory that transfers between application domains rather than a separate model for each system. The common thread is feedback that is shared across decisions, delayed, or shaped by operational constraints.

Approach

Probability, statistics, combinatorics, and dynamic programming. Used to connect model structure to algorithms, regret bounds, and comparative statics.
